# Scene-specific guidance

## Landscape
- Represent believable foreground, midground, background and atmospheric depth when the composition supports it.
- Respect terrain, water flow, vegetation distribution, horizon/verticals, weather, time of day and natural shadow direction.
- Reflections depend on water orientation, wave roughness, camera viewpoint and light direction.
- Resist default dramatic fog, implausible moon size, duplicate hills, impossible cloud symmetry or over-processed golden-hour gradients.
- Compositions may use leading lines, layers, negative space, off-center horizons or minimalism; no single composition rule is compulsory.
- Panorama/stitched composites must maintain coherent seams and object continuity. Explain composite technique if relevant.

## Street and documentary
- Pick one meaningful, situated moment: what is happening, what makes it specific, what the camera happens to notice.
- Include plausible local architecture, signs, vehicles, weather, tools, clothing, rhythms and activity. Avoid stock-tourism stereotypes.
- Unposed does not mean dirty. Ordinary imperfections may be present but never manufactured as a cliché.
- Bystanders should have independent body language; avoid everyone looking at the lens or moving identically.
- Hands, tools and work processes must reflect plausible physics and actual use.
- A subtle gesture, silhouette, shadow or empty space can tell a story without front-facing faces.
- Motion, blur and illumination must match exposure and real sources.
- Do not present an invented staged scene as factual journalism.

## Fine-art emotional realism
- Allow deliberate staging, symbolic objects, balanced symmetry, poetic light and nonliteral emotion, while keeping materials and spatial relationships credible.
- Choose a single emotional anchor, not a stack of rain, neon, melancholy, fog, coffee cup, handwritten letter and flowers by default.
- Keep photographic plausibility without insisting on a bland smartphone aesthetic.

## Local-cultural accuracy
- Use requested geography; do not automatically make all scenes Vietnamese.
- For Vietnam: prioritize contextually plausible housing, streets, local vegetation, market objects and cultural practices for the chosen region and time.
- If exact landmarks/signage are essential, request or use verified references; avoid confidently fabricating their details.
- Depict people respectfully without assuming a gender, ethnicity or age unless user/profile requests it.

## Photographer playbooks — additional genres (v1.1)

These are **optional scene-specific patterns**, not fixed templates.

### Composition and subject relationship
- Establish a visual focal anchor where the story needs one; leading lines, framing, contrast and focus falloff may direct attention without mandatory symmetry or thirds.
- Rule of thirds or centered symmetry are **options**. A deliberate alternative can be more authentic than repeating one formula.
- Give gaze/motion room when it strengthens the composition; intentional tension or tight crops can also work.
- Anchor physical scale using objects or environmental cues when ambiguity would make the scene implausible.

### Portrait and human subjects
- Choose eye-level or intentionally elevated/low camera placement; maintain plausible posture, balance, weight shift, anatomy, joints and hand grips.
- Facial expression should arise from the situation; avoid uniform marketing smiles and unnaturally identical faces.
- Skin texture and clothing tension/folds should match lighting, age, materials and action. Do not artificially force pores into distant faces.

### Product, packaging and still life
- Respect objects' true weight, dimensions, contact/occlusion, seams, printable label surfaces, material-specific roughness and edge thickness.
- Reflective/glossy finishes need plausible highlight shapes and environmental reflections, not pasted-on gradients.
- Exact logos/text should be verified on the rendered asset, not assumed accurate because the prompt says "exact".

### Architecture and interiors
- Keep room geometry, construction, furniture scale and walkable clearances credible. Verticals may converge naturally with camera tilt; plumb perspective is not always required.
- Windows/practical lamps should contribute correctly to light direction, color and shadow softness. Avoid showing every interior surface equally bright.

### Food and beverage
- Show surface moisture, steam, crumbs, pores and texture **only where temperature, humidity and materials justify them**.
- Vessels, utensils and ingredients must look physically supported, weighted and appropriately scaled; avoid glossy plastic-looking food.
- Frame for appetizing visual storytelling without assuming a coffee cup, bokeh, window light or stock-ad perfection.
